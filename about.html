About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Economics at HEC Montréal with extensive experience in applied microeconomics, labor economics, and development policy analysis.

I combine causal inference and economic modeling to study the effects of economic policies and shocks.

Education

  • Ph.D. in Applied Economics, HEC Montréal, Canada (2020–2026)

    • Dissertation completed; defence expected Fall 2026. Main supervisor: Decio Coviello.
    References: Decio Coviello, Fabian Lange, and Ismael Mourifié.

  • M.Sc. in Statistics and Economics, ENSEA Abidjan, Côte d’Ivoire (2013–2016)

    • Advanced training in econometrics and quantitative methods for applied economic research.

Teaching Experience

  • Teaching Assistant – HEC Montréal

    • MATH80816: Applied Econometrics and Statistical Learning for Economics (Graduate Level), Summer 2023.

I led tutorials, graded assignments, and supported student learning in applied econometrics and causal inference methods.

Professional Experience (Prior to the Ph.D.)

Before starting my Ph.D., I worked as an Economist Consultant at the World Bank (2018–2020), contributing to fiscal incidence, inequality, and social protection analyses across Sub-Saharan Africa, Europe, and Central Asia.

  • Coauthored World Bank Policy Research Papers on Mali and Niger (2019).
  • Led microdata harmonization and fiscal microsimulation for multiple African countries.
  • Provided technical assistance and training for Ministries of Finance and Statistics Offices.

Earlier, I was a Research Analyst at the Ministry of Planning and Development in Abidjan (2017–2018), and an Economist at Bloomfield Investment Corporation (2016–2017), where I produced country and sector risk assessments.
